03OOH & copy words in the wild

Copy is just listening, written down.

Before I write a word I want the insight: who's reading, what they already believe, and the words they'd use themselves. Someone behind the wheel gets three seconds with a billboard. Someone walking past a citylight gets a few steps. Someone scrolling gets one beat of a caption or on‑screen text. So every line has to feel like it was written for one person, in that place, at that moment.

06About me, briefly

Making things has come naturally for as long as I can remember: stories, visuals, style, music. While my friends were planning their next bit of trouble (not that I skipped mine), I already knew I wanted to make things with a soul, a style and a punch. I started in PR, where I learned how brands think and how to make people feel something, fast. Then I realised I'd rather make the story than pitch it.

Now I write, come up with concepts and hunt for the point that stays in your head. The dream is leading creative projects at a film studio. Until then: a good team, a brief worth losing sleep over, and something people actually remember.

How I work start to finish

  1. The ideaFinding the f*cking spark.
  2. The scriptWords first, then the blueprint: shot list, schedule, call sheet and crew.
  3. The copyThe line on your cereal box, the billboard you notice on your commute, the caption you don't skip. You feel it.
  4. The lookVisualising the direction and briefing the designer, so the pitch lands exactly as imagined and looks good doing it.
  5. Faces, places and propsCasting, scouting and styling. Finding the faces, spaces and details that make the story real.
  6. The setDirecting on set: standing between the monitor and the camera to bring the vision home.
  8. The final cut & afterGuiding post‑production until every frame hits. Then staying on after release: community management, the comments and how it really lands.
